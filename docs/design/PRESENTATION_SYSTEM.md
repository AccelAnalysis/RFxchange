# RFxchange Presentation System

**Status: CANONICAL PRESENTATION / DECK VISUAL AUTHORITY**

This file normalizes the approved brand-and-presentation deck into durable rules for RFxchange presentations. Product UI rules live in `RFxchange_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`; presentation point sizes and slide compositions in this file should not be copied directly into application CSS.

---

## 1. Presentation objective

RFxchange presentations should communicate the network as connective economic infrastructure that makes local business activity **visible, connected and actionable**.

The presentation feel is:

- civic trust;
- market intelligence;
- premium local network;
- decisive but measured;
- polished and uncluttered.

The presentation should not feel like a generic software dashboard, flashy startup pitch or promotional collage.

---

## 2. Identity

Use the black-and-gold RFxchange identity consistently.

### Wordmark

- `RF` in RF Gold;
- `xchange` in Exchange Black on light backgrounds;
- reverse `xchange` to white/Warm Ivory on dark backgrounds;
- restrained `™` unless a governed legal update changes trademark status;
- no logo gradients;
- no decorative outline around the wordmark.

### Recommended title

**The RFxchange: A Local Business Growth Network**

---

## 3. Presentation color system

Core palette:

- Exchange Black `#0B0B0D`;
- RF Gold `#D6A23A`;
- Warm Ivory `#F7F3EA`;
- Graphite `#252932`;
- Signal Blue `#2E5EAA`;
- Growth Green `#3B7B57`.

Directional composition target from the approved deck:

- 70% light/dark canvas;
- 20% black/graphite structure;
- 7% gold accent;
- 3% data colors.

Use gold to guide attention. Do not decorate every slide or element with gold.

Warm Ivory is the preferred light canvas. Exchange Black/Graphite are the preferred section-divider/dark-canvas family.

---

## 4. Typography

### Families

- Aptos Display — deck title, slide titles, major promises;
- Aptos — body, captions, data labels, speaker-support text.

Use system-safe fallbacks when Aptos is unavailable. Do not package font files merely to preserve the appearance.

### Slide hierarchy

- Deck title: `42–54 pt`;
- Slide title: `30–36 pt`;
- Body: `17–22 pt`;
- Footnotes/sources/caveats: `6.5–8 pt`.

Guidance:

- one-line title where possible;
- slide title should express one thought rather than a sentence stack;
- body should use sharp phrases rather than dense paragraphs;
- footnotes are for sources/caveats/claims discipline, not hidden body copy.

---

## 5. Visual language

Make the invisible economic network visible.

Preferred visual devices:

- map-first geographic context;
- released/controlled locality treatments;
- real entity markers;
- service territories;
- connection paths;
- complete business journeys;
- measured data/intelligence;
- actual product screenshots when available.

Avoid:

- generic handshakes;
- decorative city skylines;
- overfilled dashboards;
- stock imagery with no connection to the use case;
- speculative charts that imply unsupported outcomes;
- promotional content blocks unrelated to the slide's decision.

---

## 6. Golden paths

RF Gold connection paths can explain movement through the network:

- opportunity issuance;
- organization discovery;
- teaming;
- referral/resource routing;
- response;
- award/outcome;
- network-effect explanation.

A gold path should represent a meaningful relationship or sequence. Do not use gold lines as ambient decoration.

---

## 7. Shared audience spine

Every audience deck should tell the same core story, then change emphasis.

1. Fragmented local economy.
2. Why fragmentation costs growth/coordination.
3. The RFxchange solution.
4. Complete business journey.
5. Trust and data boundaries.
6. Audience-specific value.
7. Pilot / launch pathway.
8. Participation request.

The shared story is that local businesses, buyers, resource providers, opportunities, referrals and intelligence should not remain scattered across disconnected lists, meetings, spreadsheets and websites.

Audience adaptation changes the `why it matters`; it does not change the product identity or visual behavior.

## 9. Slide composition system

Use one strong composition per slide.

### Hero statement

- one major claim or positioning line;
- one strong map/network/product visual;
- minimal supporting copy.

### Journey slide

- one pathway;
- roughly 5–7 major stages where appropriate;
- use map/path/sequence to make handoffs understandable.

### Evidence slide

- one central statistic/finding;
- source/context;
- sober implication;
- avoid unsupported causal leap.

### CTA slide

- specific ask;
- clear next action;
- audience-specific implication;
- do not clutter with multiple unrelated calls to action.

### Product demonstration slide

- use actual screenshot/video frame where possible;
- focus on the user decision or workflow shown;
- crop/scale for readability rather than presenting an unreadable full UI;
- label conceptual mockups explicitly if the shown feature is not live.

---

## 10. Presentation map visuals

Presentation maps should follow `MAP_VISUAL_SYSTEM.md`.

In particular:

- selected locality prominent;
- surrounding context muted;
- markers anchored to actual/representative geography rather than randomly scattered decoration;
- localities shown by boundaries/areas rather than pin icons;
- service areas shown as territory overlays;
- connection paths correspond to the narrated journey;
- do not use an outline as the routine marker/pin treatment.

When using a conceptual map to explain a future workflow, make the conceptual status clear and avoid suggesting live density/availability that does not exist.

---

## 11. Message discipline

Preferred language includes:

- connective economic infrastructure;
- capability-based discovery;
- qualified routing;
- complete business journey;
- measured pilot outcomes;
- local business growth network;
- economic intelligence.

Avoid claims such as:

- guaranteed leads;
- guaranteed awards/contracts;
- `we will create jobs` as a certain direct outcome;
- one referral pays for membership;
- replaces your CRM;
- the only platform you need;
- universal qualification.

Use causal propositions, pilot hypotheses and outcome claims carefully, especially around revenue, financing, contract awards and jobs.

Approved close from the current deck:

**Be found. Find opportunity. Build the connection.**

---

## 12. Data and evidence

- Cite sources for external statistics and consequential factual claims.
- Show timeframe/geography where it changes interpretation.
- Use Signal Blue for intelligence/data emphasis.
- Use Growth Green for positive measured outcome/progress, not to make every favorable number green.
- Keep charts simple enough to understand during presentation viewing distance.
- Avoid tiny legends or dense multi-series charts.

---

## 13. Screenshots and product imagery

Use current product UI as the preferred visual proof.

Rules:

- capture at high resolution/retina density;
- avoid stretched or blurred screenshots;
- crop to the meaningful interaction;
- maintain recognizable product branding;
- remove/private-redact sensitive data;
- use conceptual prototypes only when clearly labeled and when no current product surface can tell the story.

---

## 14. Motion and builds

Presentation motion should explain sequence, not create spectacle.

Appropriate:

- reveal stages in a business journey;
- draw a connection path as the narrative progresses;
- move camera/focus between relevant geographies;
- reveal one evidence layer at a time.

Avoid:

- constant floating/glowing;
- gratuitous object spins;
- repeated bouncing pins;
- motion that implies product functionality not actually available.

---

## 15. Legal and trademark discipline

Use `™` until a governed legal update authorizes another trademark mark.

Do not visually represent resource/provider affiliation, government endorsement, verification or commercial partnership beyond the actual approved state.

---

## 16. Relationship to product design

Presentation visuals may simplify product UI for storytelling, but they should remain recognizable as the same RFxchange design language.

The presentation system may not redefine product-state semantics. For example:

- Verification still differs from authority;
- paid membership still does not equal credibility;
- map release state still reflects actual product authority;
- a conceptual journey is not evidence of a completed live transaction.

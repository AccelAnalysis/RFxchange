# RFxchange Content and Messaging System

**Status: PLANNING AUTHORITY — CUSTOMER-FACING LANGUAGE MUST REMAIN TRUTHFUL TO CURRENT PRODUCT AND DOMAIN STATE**

## 1. Voice

The RFxchange voice is:

- direct;
- calm;
- authoritative;
- human;
- specific;
- evidence-aware;
- oriented toward the next action.

The product should sound like a capable guide that understands business activity and institutional boundaries. It should not sound like a marketing robot, a government form, a social network, or an automated procurement decision-maker.

## 2. Core message architecture

### Product

**The RFxchange**

### Category

**A Local Business Growth Network**

### Parent endorsement

**By Accel Analysis**

### Core promise

**Be found. Find opportunity. Build the connection.**

### Experience framework

**Visible. Connected. Actionable.**

### North-star statement

**A local market becoming visible and useful around every participating organization.**

## 3. Copy pattern

Whenever practical, customer-facing copy should answer:

1. **What happened?**
2. **Why does it matter?**
3. **What can the participant do next?**

Example:

> **Your business is now visible in Norfolk.**
> Add your primary capabilities to improve discovery.

Avoid messages that merely announce success or failure without meaning or recovery.

## 4. Terminology

### Preferred

- organization;
- business;
- capability;
- opportunity;
- RFx;
- issuer;
- responder;
- buyer;
- teammate;
- resource provider;
- referral;
- service territory;
- locality;
- potential match;
- verified;
- confirmed;
- reported outcome;
- next action;
- saved search;
- first-value path.

### Use carefully

- qualified: only for defined criteria and context;
- trusted: only for the governed credibility family/state;
- verified: only for the specific information/process confirmed;
- recommended: requires an actual recommendation rule and explanation;
- available: requires current authoritative availability or a clear qualification;
- local: specify locality/region/service area when ambiguity matters;
- real-time: use only when the data and delivery actually meet that standard;
- AI: name a real implemented function and its limits rather than using it as generic positioning.

### Avoid in customer-facing product copy

- controlled Exchange;
- leverage our AI-powered ecosystem;
- unlock limitless opportunities;
- guaranteed leads;
- guaranteed growth;
- something went wrong;
- invalid request;
- no data;
- seamless when the process has material boundaries;
- qualified organization as a universal label;
- trusted partner unless the governed evidence supports it;
- congratulations after ordinary actions;
- social engagement terms such as followers, likes, streaks, viral, or trending unless a future governed product intentionally requires them.

## 5. Public marketing copy

### Hero

Eyebrow:

`A LOCAL BUSINESS GROWTH NETWORK`

Headline:

`The local market is already moving. Make it visible.`

Supporting line:

`Discover capabilities, circulate opportunities, build teams, route referrals, and turn local activity into actionable intelligence.`

Primary action:

`Explore The RFxchange`

Secondary action:

`Sign in`

### Narrative chapters

#### The market is fragmented

Explain that organizations, buyers, opportunities, and providers exist across separate systems and relationships. Do not claim that no infrastructure exists or that RFxchange replaces every system.

#### See the network

Describe real implemented visibility. Do not display invented companies, opportunities, statistics, or activity represented as live evidence.

#### Follow one complete journey

Use a clearly labeled conceptual or synthetic journey until real, permissioned product evidence is available.

#### Choose your entry point

- Business: be found and act on useful demand.
- Resource provider: become discoverable in the context of a business need.
- Economic/community leader: understand how organizations, opportunities, providers, and localities connect.

#### Trust what you see

Explain provenance, organization control, permissions, credibility, and outcome distinctions without implying universal verification.

#### Enter the Exchange

Use clear join/sign-in language and truthful launch status. Do not exaggerate scarcity, network density, or commercial benefits.

## 6. Onboarding copy

### Account establishment

Use:

- `Create your account`
- `Verify your email`
- `Resume setup`
- `Your information has been saved.`

Avoid:

- `Unlock your ecosystem`
- `Start networking`
- `Almost there!` when several mandatory stages remain
- internal lifecycle language

### Geography

Preferred:

- `Where is your organization based?`
- `Search for a city or county`
- `Home locality`
- `You can explore other participating areas after setup.`

Do not imply that the business can operate only inside its home locality.

### Organization resolution

Preferred:

- `Find your organization`
- `Claim this organization`
- `Create a new organization`
- `This information came from a public source.`
- `Review and correct the details before continuing.`
- `This may be a duplicate of an existing organization.`

### Marker activation

Primary:

> **Your business is now on The RFxchange.**

Contextual version:

> **Your business is now visible in Norfolk.**
> Add your primary capabilities to improve discovery.

Do not say that the business is verified, qualified, endorsed, procurement-ready, or preferred merely because the marker is active.

### First value

Use action-oriented choices:

- Find an opportunity.
- Issue an opportunity.
- Find customers or suppliers.
- Find a teammate.
- Send or receive a referral.
- Find resources and support.
- Explore the network.

If a destination is not live, say so clearly and preserve the participant’s intent without fabricating functionality.

## 7. Match and opportunity copy

### Potential match

Preferred:

> **Strong capability alignment**
> 8 of 10 identified requirements match your organization profile. One requirement needs confirmation. One required capability is not represented in your profile.

Required boundary:

A match is not qualification, endorsement, eligibility, profitability, or award likelihood.

### Incomplete match

> **You meet 7 of 9 identified requirements.**
> One requirement needs confirmation. One may require a teammate.

Actions may include:

- Review the requirement.
- Find a teammate.
- Find a provider.
- Update your profile.
- Watch this opportunity.
- Decline.

### No supplier match

> **No participating organization currently satisfies all required capabilities.**
> Review the criteria or broaden the search geography.

Do not say “no suppliers exist” unless the search represents the full authoritative market, which the Exchange generally does not.

### External submission

> **Prepare here. Submit through the issuer’s required system.**

Do not represent an external response as formally submitted when RFxchange does not control the receiving system.

## 8. Resource-provider copy

### No provider match

> **No listed provider currently meets all of these conditions.**
> Expand the service area or remove one optional preference.

### Unknown availability

Use:

> `Current availability has not been confirmed.`

Do not infer that a listed provider is accepting clients or can meet a deadline.

### Referral status

Use explicit stages:

- Proposed
- Accepted
- Contact initiated
- Service or conversation begun
- Closed
- Outcome reported

Avoid success language before a meaningful outcome is recorded.

### Stalled handoff

> **The receiving organization has not accepted this referral.**
> Send a reminder or choose another provider.

## 9. Empty states

### Opportunities

> **No current opportunities match these criteria.**
> Save the search, broaden the geography, or remove one optional filter.

### Referrals

> **No referrals yet.**
> Open an organization profile to create a structured introduction.

### Teaming

> **No potential teammate meets every selected condition.**
> Review which capability is mandatory or expand the service area.

### Resources

> **No provider is currently listed for this need in your selected area.**
> Search the regional service territory or notify the Exchange team of the gap.

### Intelligence

> **Not enough participating activity exists to display this pattern responsibly.**

Do not replace low sample size with fake examples that appear live.

## 10. Loading and autosave

### Loading

Use specific language for long operations:

- `Loading organizations in Norfolk…`
- `Checking the address…`
- `Preparing your response workspace…`
- `Recalculating matches…`

Do not show false percentages.

### Autosave

Preferred states:

- `Saving…`
- `Saved just now`
- `Could not save. Your changes remain on this device.`
- `Reconnect to save these changes.`

Do not imply server persistence when only local state exists.

## 11. Error and recovery copy

### Structure

A useful error states:

- what succeeded;
- what failed;
- whether work was preserved;
- next action;
- support/escalation only when useful.

### Map refresh failure

> **Your information was saved, but the map could not refresh.**
> Retry without re-entering your profile.

### Permission

> **You do not have permission to perform this action for this organization.**
> Ask an organization administrator for the appropriate access.

### Expired context

> **This invitation is no longer active.**
> Sign in to view the organization or request a new invitation.

### Unavailable destination

> **This pathway is not available yet.**
> Your selection has been saved so the Exchange can return you here when the workflow opens.

Only use that copy when the intent is actually persisted.

### Technical fallback

Avoid `Something went wrong.`

Use the narrowest truthful explanation available without exposing sensitive internal detail.

## 12. Success copy

Success should be proportional to the event.

### Routine save

`Profile updated.`

### Submission

`Response submitted at 2:43 p.m.`

### Marker activation

`Your business is now on The RFxchange.`

### Outcome

`Outcome recorded.` or `Outcome verified.` depending on provenance.

Do not use `Congratulations!` for every completion. Reserve warmer acknowledgment for substantive firsts or verified milestones.

## 13. Credibility language

### Verified

Use only when the defined evidence process has completed.

Standard explanation:

> Verified means that The RFxchange has confirmed the information required for the applicable verification badge. Verification does not guarantee performance, financial condition, capability for a particular engagement, or eligibility for a specific opportunity.

### Endorsed

> Endorsed indicates that the identified Verified Organization has elected to endorse this organization. An endorsement represents the endorser’s assessment and does not constitute a guarantee by The RFxchange or verification of every claim.

### Trusted

> Trusted badges reflect qualifying historical activity and feedback within The RFxchange. They describe past Exchange interactions and do not guarantee future performance.

### Qualified

Qualification must name the context and criteria. Do not create a universal Qualified Organization label.

### Recognition

Founding Organization and other Recognition markers describe historical or participation status. They do not imply verification, reliability, qualification, procurement readiness, or preferred search treatment.

## 14. Data provenance language

Where relevant, distinguish among:

- `Seeded public data`
- `Business-claimed information`
- `Provider-verified information`
- `Platform-observed activity`
- `Self-reported outcome`
- `Counterparty-confirmed outcome`
- `Independently verified outcome`

Do not collapse all provenance into `verified`.

## 15. Notifications

Organize notification language around the economic object.

Example:

**Facilities-services RFx**

- Addendum issued.
- One teammate document is outstanding.
- Submission closes Friday at 3:00 p.m.

Avoid undifferentiated messages such as `You have 3 new notifications.` when the object and urgency can be stated.

Notifications must not use artificial urgency, fear, or engagement pressure.

## 16. Commercial and membership language

Membership and credibility remain separate.

Do not say:

- paid members are more trusted;
- Founding members are preferred suppliers;
- membership improves legitimate match scoring;
- one referral will pay for membership;
- joining guarantees leads, contracts, financing, or growth.

Use:

> `Founding Organization is a recognition of participation in the launch cohort. It is not a verification, qualification, endorsement, or guarantee of business results.`

Commercial benefits must be active, approved, and described exactly.

## 17. Claims discipline

### Strong product claims

The product may claim implemented capabilities such as:

- capability-based discovery;
- contextual opportunity visibility;
- structured referral status;
- service-territory discovery;
- map-based organization visibility;
- saved searches and alerts;
- governed credibility evidence.

### Causal propositions

Use measured language:

- `designed to improve`;
- `can help reduce`;
- `may increase the likelihood`;
- `supports`;
- `makes visible`;
- `helps participants identify`.

### Hypotheses and outcomes

Do not claim fixed increases in revenue, jobs, contracts, provider efficiency, procurement participation, or economic resilience without appropriate evidence.

Use pilot and measurement language where causality is not established.

## 18. Synthetic tutorial language

Every tutorial must make its status clear:

- `Interactive example`
- `Tutorial scenario`
- `No live opportunity or organization record is being created.`

Avoid fictional organization names that resemble real local firms unless clearly isolated and approved. Tutorial data must not appear in public screenshots without the tutorial label.

## 19. Accessibility and readability

- use plain language;
- explain abbreviations on first use where appropriate;
- do not rely on icon labels alone for consequential actions;
- keep link text descriptive;
- use headings that describe the task;
- write validation near the field and summarize when necessary;
- use status labels rather than color alone;
- avoid all-caps paragraphs;
- keep time/date language localizable and unambiguous;
- distinguish error, warning, information, and success semantically.

## 20. Review checklist

Before customer-facing copy ships, confirm:

- it names the product consistently;
- it is truthful to the current domain state;
- it does not invent activity or evidence;
- it distinguishes potential match from qualification;
- it distinguishes activation, credibility, recognition, membership, and outcome;
- it says what happened, why it matters, and the next action;
- it preserves institutional and legal boundaries;
- it is understandable to a business owner without internal terminology;
- it has an accessible label and localization path;
- it avoids exaggerated, addictive, or manipulative language.

# The RFxchange Brand Experience System

**Status: PLANNING AUTHORITY — IMPLEMENT THROUGH AN APPROVED POST-WAVE-2 CONVERGENCE GATE**

## 1. Governing idea

The RFxchange should feel like a **living market**, not a conventional dashboard.

The product should make local economic activity feel as though it is coming into focus around the participant: organizations become visible, demand becomes legible, gaps become actionable, relationships become traceable, and outcomes become evidence.

The governing brand statement is:

> **The RFxchange makes local economic activity visible, connected, and actionable.**

The north-star expression is:

> **A local market becoming visible and useful around every participating organization.**

The experience must be premium but not flashy; executive enough for civic leaders, practical enough for business owners, and trustworthy enough for resource providers.

Brand is not a logo placed over unrelated screens. It is the combined effect of:

- positioning and claims;
- visual identity;
- real geography and real entities;
- information hierarchy;
- interaction quality;
- motion continuity;
- optional sonic feedback;
- human, evidence-aware language;
- useful empty and error states;
- accessibility and sensory control;
- state preservation;
- responsiveness;
- performance;
- data provenance;
- reliable authorization and recovery.

## 2. Brand architecture

### 2.1 Product name

**The RFxchange**

Use the product name consistently. Do not alternate among Hi-Coworking Exchange, RFx Exchange, RF Exchange, RFxchange platform, or unrelated category names in customer-facing product copy unless a governed legal or corporate context requires it.

### 2.2 Category descriptor

**A Local Business Growth Network**

The descriptor explains the category without reducing the product to a directory, social network, procurement portal, coworking platform, economic-development dashboard, referral system, or resource directory.

### 2.3 Parent endorsement

**By Accel Analysis**

Use secondarily in:

- public footer and company information;
- legal and policy surfaces;
- selected launch communications;
- approved presentation and partnership materials;
- product provenance where appropriate.

The endorsement must not compete with the product wordmark or imply that participant organizations are Accel Analysis clients, partners, or endorsed entities.

### 2.4 Core promise

> **Be found. Find opportunity. Build the connection.**

### 2.5 Internal experience framework

#### Visible

A participant can see, subject to permission, release, privacy, and lifecycle rules:

- their organization;
- other organizations and capabilities;
- opportunities and structured demand;
- service territories;
- buyers and issuers;
- resource providers;
- relationships and interaction status;
- locality and regional activity;
- evidence-backed outcomes and aggregate intelligence.

#### Connected

A participant can move through governed workflows from:

- capability to buyer;
- opportunity to responder;
- business need to provider;
- capability gap to potential teammate;
- referral to receiving organization;
- RFx to response;
- activity to reported outcome.

#### Actionable

The platform should not stop at visibility. Where the domain is implemented and the participant is authorized, it provides:

- a clear next action;
- an understandable workflow;
- a responsible party;
- a status;
- a deadline where relevant;
- a measurable result or evidence state.

### 2.6 Audience outcome

| Audience | Intended feeling |
| --- | --- |
| Business owner | “My organization is visible, and I can act on something useful.” |
| Resource provider | “The right businesses can find us, and referrals arrive with appropriate context.” |
| Economic or community leader | “I can see how businesses, opportunities, providers, and localities connect.” |

One brand serves all three audiences. The interface may change emphasis, permissions, density, and narrative, but it must not become three unrelated products.

## 3. Brand behavior

The RFxchange should feel:

- grounded;
- local without being geographically limiting;
- intelligent without appearing speculative;
- authoritative without becoming bureaucratic;
- human without becoming casual;
- premium without resembling a luxury lifestyle product;
- active without becoming noisy;
- evidence-aware without becoming cold.

Avoid experiences that resemble:

- a generic social feed;
- a consumer directory;
- a cryptocurrency or speculative-finance product;
- a neon cyber interface;
- a procurement portal with a branded header;
- a government form system;
- a crowded enterprise dashboard;
- a gamified engagement platform.

## 4. Visual identity

### 4.1 Wordmark

- `RF` uses RF Gold.
- `xchange` uses Exchange Black on light surfaces and Warm Ivory/white on intentional dark surfaces.
- Use the governed trademark mark.
- Do not place gradients, glow, outlines, bevels, or shadows inside the wordmark.
- Keep the parent endorsement visually secondary.

### 4.2 Color system

| Color | Hex | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exchange Black | `#0B0B0D` | authority, primary typography, major navigation, cinematic framing |
| Warm Ivory | `#F7F3EA` | default participant canvas, clarity, warmth, human accessibility |
| Graphite | `#252932` | panels, dense information, secondary structure, dark-mode surfaces |
| RF Gold | `#D6A23A` | connection, focus, selected action, signature brand signal |
| Accessible Dark Gold | `#8A6418` | small gold-family text on light surfaces when contrast requires it |
| Signal Blue | `#2E5EAA` | intelligence, active data, opportunities, links, discovery |
| Growth Green | `#3B7B57` | completed actions, reported outcomes, positive resolution |

The important requirement is semantic enforcement, not palette expansion.

#### Gold means connection and focus

Gold may identify:

- `RF` in the wordmark;
- selected or primary actions;
- active paths between organizations;
- locality focus;
- marker activation;
- short eyebrow rules or labels;
- restrained strategic emphasis.

Gold must not automatically mean:

- paid;
- better;
- verified;
- more trustworthy;
- higher ranked;
- sponsored;
- awarded;
- qualified.

Membership, sponsorship, recognition, credibility, qualification, and outcome remain separate concepts.

`#D6A23A` is appropriate for lines, icons, large accents, and selected surfaces. It is not the default small-text color on Warm Ivory. Use Accessible Dark Gold or Graphite with a gold rule where necessary.

#### Light is the default

The ordinary participant environment uses:

- Warm Ivory and light geographic foundations;
- Graphite or black structure;
- translucent overlays where context should remain visible;
- restrained gold;
- Signal Blue data and opportunity cues;
- Growth Green only for genuine positive resolution or outcome meaning.

Black creates contrast and ceremony. It is not the default full-screen participant background.

### 4.3 Typography

The product should use two semantic type roles.

#### Display role

A distinctive geometric variable sans serif may be selected and licensed for:

- marketing headlines;
- major section titles;
- onboarding milestones;
- outcome statements;
- presentation-mode labels.

It must feel modern and authoritative, not futuristic, playful, excessively rounded, condensed, or ornamental.

#### Interface role

A high-legibility sans serif must support:

- large x-height;
- clear differentiation among similar characters;
- reliable rendering at 12–16px;
- tabular numerals;
- clear weight hierarchy;
- broad character and accessibility support.

Aptos remains an acceptable system-safe baseline and fallback. Do not bundle unlicensed font files.

#### Typographic behavior

- Use sentence case by default.
- Reserve uppercase for short eyebrow or status labels with adequate tracking.
- Avoid long centered paragraphs.
- Do not use thin type over maps or photography.
- Use tabular numerals for prices, dates, deadlines, counts, and evaluation data.
- Keep statuses concise and human-readable.
- Use hierarchy, spacing, and grouping before adding borders or decorative containers.

## 5. Proprietary visual grammar

RFxchange screenshots should remain recognizable when the wordmark is absent.

The product uses a consistent market grammar:

> **Nodes are participants. Beacons are demand. Paths are interactions. Fields are geography or service coverage. Seals are evidence. Green resolutions are outcomes.**

The canonical object system is defined in `MAP_AND_DATA_VISUAL_GRAMMAR.md` and includes:

- organization nodes;
- opportunity beacons;
- service fields;
- golden paths;
- outcome paths;
- credibility seals;
- locality fields;
- proprietary density and heatmap treatment.

No customer-facing map or screenshot may use arbitrary markers, rainbow category colors, invented geographic meaning, generic white marker outlines, or decorative connection lines that do not correspond to a real or clearly identified synthetic tutorial state.

## 6. Marketing experience

The marketing site is the public entrance into the same product, not a separate campaign microsite.

### 6.1 Hero direction

Use a full-bleed real photograph or short muted video showing:

- an actual entrepreneur, buyer, resource provider, or project team;
- an actual RFxchange screen only when presentation-ready;
- recognizable local or regional context where available;
- sufficient negative space for concise overlay copy;
- a dark readability gradient rather than a boxed text panel.

Recommended public structure:

- Eyebrow: `A LOCAL BUSINESS GROWTH NETWORK`
- Headline: `The local market is already moving. Make it visible.`
- Supporting line: `Discover capabilities, circulate opportunities, build teams, route referrals, and turn local activity into actionable intelligence.`
- Primary action: `Explore The RFxchange`
- Secondary action: `Sign in`

### 6.2 Narrative chapters

The public page should use a small number of immersive chapters:

1. The market is fragmented.
2. See the network.
3. Follow one complete journey.
4. Choose your entry point.
5. Trust what you see.
6. Enter the Exchange.

The directional balance is approximately 70% visual and 30% copy.

### 6.3 Evidence and asset integrity

Do not advertise the product with invented:

- organizations;
- opportunities;
- maps;
- statistics;
- outcomes;
- testimonials;
- buyer activity;
- provider availability;
- dashboards represented as real product evidence.

A synthetic tutorial or conceptual diagram may be used only when it is clearly labeled as such and cannot be mistaken for live platform activity.

Photography should emphasize:

- businesses performing real work;
- buyers reviewing real needs;
- resource providers advising firms;
- entrepreneurs using technology in operational settings;
- manufacturing, construction, professional services, logistics, creative work, and other real sectors;
- regional geography and infrastructure;
- collaborative decision moments.

Avoid handshakes, meaningless charts, unrelated skylines, fake interfaces composited into screens, invented testimonials, and overly staged stock-team imagery.

Bottom-matter links must open dedicated pages rather than scrolling to hidden sections for policies, company information, support, accessibility, and other required material.

## 7. Onboarding as the first product demonstration

The first success moment is not “registration complete.” It is:

> **Your business is now visible in The RFxchange.**

### 7.1 Account establishment

- registration appears over a subdued edge-to-edge regional map when geography is relevant;
- Warm Ivory panel;
- black/Graphite typography;
- gold progress signal;
- real policy links;
- no duplicated questions;
- no unnecessary participation-role requirements;
- state preserved through policy review, email verification, sign-out, sign-in, and recovery.

### 7.2 Geography selection

- accessible locality typeahead;
- map begins orienting only when confidence and interaction rules permit;
- selected locality boundary becomes visible;
- surrounding localities become subdued rather than disappearing;
- the experience explains that geography is operating context, not a permanent wall around the business.

### 7.3 Organization resolution

Potential organizations appear as anchored candidates from authoritative or permitted records.

The participant must understand:

- which information is seeded;
- which information is claimed or supplied by the organization;
- what can be corrected;
- whether a possible duplicate exists;
- what authority is required to claim or create the organization.

### 7.4 Address and marker placement

The spatial transition communicates continuity:

1. locality focuses;
2. address resolves;
3. street/building detail becomes clearer;
4. organization marker arrives;
5. one soft gold radius settles.

The current camera signature remains subject to the authoritative spatial contract:

- locality view: approximately 225-second ambient orbit, 60-degree pitch, zoomed to fit;
- organization view: approximately 225-second ambient orbit, 75-degree pitch, zoom 16;
- orbit is user-toggleable and disabled or replaced under reduced-motion behavior.

### 7.5 Marker activation

When the marker becomes active:

- the marker settles at the real/privacy-safe coordinate;
- one restrained gold pulse travels outward;
- the locality boundary briefly resolves;
- nearby permitted layers appear progressively;
- an approved short sound may play only when sound is enabled;
- the message appears: `Your business is now on The RFxchange.`

Follow with one clear next action, such as:

> Add two capabilities so buyers and opportunities can find you more accurately.

Do not present a wall of enrichment requirements at the activation moment.

### 7.6 Interactive orientation

The orientation should demonstrate one continuous synthetic scenario:

1. an organization issues an opportunity;
2. a responder receives a potential capability match;
3. a missing capability is identified;
4. a potential teammate appears;
5. an invitation travels along a gold path;
6. the teammate accepts without implying a legal agreement;
7. a resource provider appears where support is relevant;
8. a structured response proceeds to human evaluation and outcome.

Tutorial objects must be clearly synthetic and isolated from live records.

## 8. Daily workspace

The platform should feel like one continuous environment. Opportunities, referrals, resources, teaming, credibility, and intelligence are different lenses on the same market.

### 8.1 Core structure

- edge-to-edge map where geography is the primary context;
- one restrained top navigation;
- consistent search placement;
- one filter system;
- responsive drawers and contextual panels;
- preserved map position between related lenses;
- preserved selected object when appropriate;
- clear return paths rather than modal dead ends.

### 8.2 Organization home

The organization detail experience may show, subject to implementation and permission:

- capabilities;
- service area;
- public credibility;
- current opportunities or interests;
- authorized actions;
- relationship context;
- data provenance.

### 8.3 Opportunity/RFx home

The opportunity experience should explain:

- why the opportunity appeared;
- potential match reasoning without endorsement or qualification claims;
- mandatory and preferred requirements;
- capability gaps;
- deadlines and lifecycle;
- possible teammates;
- relevant resources;
- pursue, watch, or decline choices where implemented.

### 8.4 Referral home

A referral may visibly move through:

`Proposed → Accepted → Contact initiated → Service or conversation begun → Closed → Outcome reported`

The visual state must reflect authoritative domain state and must not imply a sale or successful economic outcome merely because a referral exists.

### 8.5 Resource-provider home

A provider may be represented through:

- service territory;
- eligibility;
- availability where authoritative;
- intake method;
- relevance to the current need;
- referral status.

### 8.6 Context travels with the participant

When a capability or readiness gap appears, the product should preserve the opportunity context and offer appropriate implemented actions such as:

- find a teammate;
- find a provider;
- update the organization profile;
- confirm the requirement another way.

The interface should help complete the connection rather than merely expose the problem.

## 9. Motion

Premium motion is meaningful continuity, not constant animation.

Use the canonical categories in `MOTION_SYSTEM.md`:

- micro: 120–180ms;
- panel: 220–320ms;
- spatial: 600–1,200ms;
- milestone: approximately 2–4 seconds.

Longer motion must use smooth acceleration and deceleration and must not begin or stop abruptly.

Signature motions include locality reveal, marker lock, RFx trace, connection accepted, and outcome resolution.

Do not continuously pulse every marker, use decorative particles, animate routine table refreshes, use confetti for ordinary actions, bounce controls, or animate heatmaps without an underlying change.

## 10. Sonic identity

Sound is sparse, optional, and secondary to visible state.

The sonic language should feel warm, precise, modern, reassuring, and more like a refined instrument than a mobile game.

A three-part motif may express:

`Visible → Connected → Actionable`

Appropriate event families include:

- organization marker activated;
- invitation or referral accepted;
- RFx published or response submitted;
- verified outcome recorded;
- credibility milestone earned;
- high-priority deadline or action.

Do not use sound for page loads, hover, typing, routine filtering, ordinary navigation, or every notification.

Production sound requires approved assets, event contracts, master and category controls, silent equivalents, and accessibility acceptance. No autoplaying marketing-page audio is permitted.

## 11. Messaging

The product voice is:

- direct;
- calm;
- authoritative;
- human;
- specific;
- evidence-aware;
- oriented toward the next action.

Avoid jargon, exaggerated technology language, generic errors, unsupported percentages, and celebration after routine actions.

The governing copy pattern is:

> **What happened → why it matters → what the participant can do next.**

Examples and terminology are controlled in `CONTENT_AND_MESSAGING_SYSTEM.md`.

## 12. Empty states

Empty states are guided business actions, not blank dashboards.

They must:

- state the actual condition;
- avoid implying that the platform is broken;
- provide one to three meaningful next actions;
- remain truthful about network density and available features;
- use the same node, beacon, path, field, and seal grammar as the live product;
- avoid unrelated cartoon illustration styles.

## 13. Credibility

Credibility is a refined evidence system, not gamification.

The five substantive families remain:

- Verified;
- Active;
- Experienced;
- Trusted;
- Endorsed.

Recognition remains separate. Membership, sponsorship, Founding status, or payment cannot purchase substantive credibility or distort legitimate matching.

Badge design uses:

- common seal structure;
- distinct internal symbol;
- written family and label;
- level where applicable;
- clickable explanation;
- non-color differentiation;
- visible evidence/provenance explanation where permitted.

Badge motion is restrained: seal outline, symbol, readable label, explanation, then stop. No confetti, rankings, public numerical trust scores, or competitive points.

## 14. Recurring use

The product should create recurring value through relevance, not addiction.

The retention loop is:

`Relevant signal → Understandable context → Useful action → Visible status → Reported outcome → Better future intelligence`

Legitimate return reasons include:

- a saved search produces a new match;
- a referral changes status;
- a teammate responds;
- a provider accepts a handoff;
- a deadline approaches;
- an addendum or clarification is issued;
- a buyer invites or views the organization where such visibility is governed;
- a capability gap appears;
- a credibility milestone is earned;
- a material locality or sector pattern changes;
- a response moves to another lifecycle stage.

Avoid streaks, generic return prompts, artificial scarcity, random rewards, likes, follower counts, and engagement prompts unrelated to business value.

## 15. Viewing modes and accessibility

The governed modes are:

- **Exchange Light:** default Warm Ivory participant environment;
- **Intelligence Dark:** near-black, Graphite, or charcoal analytical environment without neon styling;
- **Presentation Mode:** controlled executive and public briefing mode, not an enlarged ordinary interface.

Accessibility and sensory controls include:

- reduced motion;
- orbit off;
- sound off;
- high contrast;
- keyboard navigation;
- visible focus;
- screen-reader equivalents for maps/charts;
- list alternatives to spatial discovery;
- labels and patterns in addition to color;
- opaque readable surfaces for dense tables and long workflows.

Glassmorphism is reserved for light contextual overlays. Dense data and long-form workflows use stable opaque surfaces.

## 16. Premium behavior is engineering behavior

Premium quality requires:

- registration resumes where the participant left it;
- prior information is carried forward;
- map state is preserved where appropriate;
- loading and validation are explained;
- autosave state is clear;
- submissions are unambiguous;
- errors preserve work;
- destructive actions explain consequences;
- each workflow includes loading, empty, success, error, permission, expired, and recovery states where applicable;
- controls do not shift after asynchronous loading;
- dense views remain responsive;
- mobile receives the same design discipline as desktop;
- public pages remain performant despite immersive media;
- authorization and server state remain authoritative over visual optimism.

The premium equation is:

> **Coherence × usefulness × trust × performance**

Gold, blur, 3D maps, sound, and animation cannot compensate for broken state, ambiguous authority, invented evidence, slow performance, or inaccessible interaction.

## 17. Implementation classification

Every brand requirement must be assigned to one of three classes.

### 17.1 Cross-cutting standard

May govern existing and future surfaces without claiming a new domain feature:

- semantic color;
- typography;
- motion timing/easing;
- messaging;
- empty/error/loading behavior;
- accessibility;
- state preservation;
- evidence integrity.

### 17.2 Net-new product capability

Requires explicit product scope, persistence, permissions, tests, and tracker treatment where appropriate:

- Intelligence Dark;
- Presentation Mode;
- sound preferences;
- notification/milestone sound controls;
- haptics;
- list-based alternatives where not already implemented;
- recurring Exchange Brief preferences.

### 17.3 Domain-dependent brand expression

May be designed now but cannot be represented as live until its authoritative domain exists:

- opportunity beacons;
- service fields;
- referrals and handoff paths;
- teaming paths;
- RFx lifecycle paths;
- credibility seals;
- provider availability;
- verified outcome paths.

## 18. Acceptance principle

A surface is not brand-complete because it uses the palette or wordmark.

It must satisfy the applicable acceptance matrix for:

- brand consistency;
- map continuity;
- responsive behavior;
- accessibility;
- motion;
- sound behavior where relevant;
- loading/empty/success/error/permission/expired states;
- recovery and state preservation;
- data provenance;
- no conflict between paid status and credibility;
- no invented customer-facing evidence;
- performance;
- domain and authorization boundaries.

## 19. North-star journey

A visitor arrives on a cinematic real-world image of entrepreneurs using or preparing to use the actual product. The language is brief and confident.

They begin registration without leaving the brand environment.

After account creation, the map opens edge to edge.

They search for their locality. Its boundary comes into focus.

They find or create their organization. The camera moves toward the confirmed location.

Their real marker appears, settles, and may produce the restrained RFxchange arrival sound when enabled.

Nearby permitted capabilities, opportunities, and resource territories emerge.

A clearly synthetic orientation demonstrates how demand can lead to a match, a gap, a teammate, a provider, a response, human evaluation, and an outcome.

The participant enters the workspace centered on their organization and receives one useful next action.

When they return, they do not meet a generic dashboard. They see what has materially changed around their business position:

- a relevant opportunity;
- a referral update;
- a teammate response;
- a provider handoff;
- a deadline;
- a verified outcome.

That is the RFxchange brand: **a local market becoming visible and useful around every participating organization.**

# RFxchange Acquisition & Retention Context

## Growth model

RFxchange should acquire participants through useful economic objects and relationships, not primarily through a generic SaaS funnel.

Core flywheels:

```text
Seed → Reach → Acquire → Activate → Deliver Value → Build Habit → Convert → Retain → Expand → Advocate
```

and

```text
More Organizations → More Capabilities → More Opportunities → More Connections → More Outcomes → Better Intelligence → More Value → More Organizations
```

## Acquisition value proposition

Broad acquisition should lead with the market-language problem and customer outcome rather than a disconnected product-feature inventory.

Businesses often know what they need without knowing the exact procurement term, provider classification or capability language that describes it. Capable providers may describe what they do differently than buyers describe what they need.

The RFxchange acquisition story should therefore move through:

```text
Understandable → Discoverable → Connectable → Actionable
```

A prospect should understand that establishing the organization and confirming useful capability/geography context creates a reusable market position; it is not merely profile completion for its own sake.

Public marketing may explain AMACS as the common structure connecting what the market needs with what organizations can do, and may explain implemented interpretation assistance with the concise principle **AI can suggest. People confirm.** Do not make taxonomy expertise or generic AI claims the acquisition hook.

RFxchange is broader than formal procurement. Avoid defining the product primarily as an RFP platform or bid portal even when a high-intent acquisition object is an RFx/opportunity.

## Market the object, not merely the platform

High-intent acquisition begins with something the participant already wants, such as:
- an opportunity,
- an organization profile waiting to be claimed,
- a referral,
- a team invitation,
- a buyer/supplier need,
- a relevant provider/resource,
- a locality/network launch.

The object-specific CTA should preserve why the visitor arrived while remaining consistent with the broader value proposition.

## Permanent marketing versus Founding campaign

The permanent RFxchange homepage and the Founding campaign have different jobs.

The permanent homepage defines the durable product value: make organizations and market needs easier to understand, discover and connect through governed market structure.

The dedicated Founding campaign surface may make a narrower conversion argument around early organization positioning, structured launch feedback, approved enhanced tools/support and historical launch-cohort recognition.

Do not temporarily redefine the permanent product around the Founding campaign. During the campaign, the permanent homepage may use an announcement, CTA or removable campaign module that routes qualified visitors to the dedicated Founding surface.

## Founding conversion

The Founding model remains organization-level and subject to the approved commercial authority. Founding participation must remain separate from neutral market legitimacy.

The preferred conversion sequence is:

```text
Campaign visitor
→ organization established
→ first useful market value
→ Founding offer
→ paid Founding Organization
```

Where a campaign visitor intentionally arrives to evaluate Founding participation, the Founding offer may be explained before activation, but payment should not become a mandatory gate to useful free participation.

Campaign messaging may emphasize:

- establish the organization and capability position early;
- participate in structured launch feedback;
- help reveal real terminology and workflow gaps that can inform governed RFxchange and AMACS refinement;
- receive Founding Organization recognition;
- receive only those enhanced tools, early-access benefits or launch-support benefits that are actually approved and available; and
- participate within the real approved cohort limit when commercial enrollment is open.

Campaign messaging must not imply:

- pay-to-rank discovery;
- pay-to-verify;
- pay-to-qualify;
- better substantive matching because of membership;
- automatic credibility;
- guaranteed referrals, leads, contracts, financing or growth; or
- that participant feedback automatically mutates the governed AMACS standard.

## Public opportunity entry

A visitor should be able to understand enough of a permitted public RFx/opportunity to become interested before registration. The CTA should preserve the object intent (for example **View Opportunity**) rather than forcing a generic join funnel.

## Preserve entry context

Registration must preserve why the person arrived. Supported entry contexts include opportunity, organization claim, referral, team invitation, provider recommendation, buyer need, Founding-campaign intent and other approved acquisition objects.

After authentication/onboarding, return the user to the relevant object or next action rather than an empty generic dashboard.

## Seed-and-claim

A seeded organization profile can function as an acquisition surface. Show appropriate public organization/location/category data, clearly label the profile as unclaimed, and allow **Claim this organization**.

The user is taking control of an organization presence, not creating an unattached personal account.

## Institutional distribution

EDOs, chambers, governments, APEX/SBDC/workforce/university partners and other approved institutions can recruit businesses and providers into a locality/network. Partner attribution must not imply that an institution owns the network or gains unauthorized access to participant data.

Founding recruitment through chambers, incubators, economic-development organizations or other partners should preserve the same distinction: the recruiting relationship may explain the cohort but cannot imply institutional endorsement, verification or privileged discovery.

## Referrals and teaming as acquisition

An external organization invited to receive a referral or discuss a team opportunity should register into that context. These are high-intent routes and should not discard the invitation after signup.

## Resource acquisition loop

Resource providers may share relevant opportunities or profile-completion paths, while businesses discover providers in context. Optimize for relevant routing and service territory/eligibility rather than lead volume.

Do not describe provider routing as “qualified leads” unless the applicable criteria and meaning of qualification are explicitly defined.

## Activation target

The activation objective is not merely registration. A successful organization should establish a legitimate organization presence and reach the real controlled-map environment quickly.

Activation messaging should help the participant understand that the organization is creating a reusable market position, not just completing account setup.

## First value

Use stated objectives and preserved acquisition context to drive the first useful action. Examples include becoming visible/discoverable by confirmed capability, finding a relevant organization, receiving or sending a referral, finding a provider, saving/pursuing an opportunity, finding a teammate or issuing demand when the applicable workflows are live.

First value should be truthful to the currently released product. Do not route a participant into a synthetic future workflow merely because it illustrates the long-term model.

## Retention

Retention should come from recurring economic usefulness: profile/capability reuse, relevant discovery, saved searches/alerts, opportunity changes, referral status, team activity, provider follow-up, issuer workflows and relevant intelligence as those domains are released.

Persistent education should reinforce why structured organization information becomes more useful across repeated interactions, rather than repeatedly teaching the same navigation mechanics.

Relevance is more important than notification volume.

## Conversion

Upgrade prompts should be contextual to demonstrated value, limits or advanced workflow needs. Do not interrupt minimum registration with premature commercial conversion.

Founding recognition and commercial entitlement remain separate from organization authority, verification, credibility and neutral market matching. Commercial conversion should deepen participation or productivity, not sell substantive market truth.
